## Relationships, notation and units

BM1–BM9 are category identifiers, not ranks, formula variables or mutually exclusive ownership labels. Source p. 14 offers six analytical lenses; the note's sequential workflow is enriched. `CLV` means customer lifetime value [standard acronym expansion]; only the learning goal names it here. No formula, discount rate, currency or horizon is specified, so do not introduce a course CLV calculation.

Value creation can support capture but does not imply it. Measurement should match the claimed benefit. An organization can instantiate more than one pattern, depending on the service/participant under discussion. Revenue examples in the note are hypotheses unless the source case specifies them.

## Visual intuition and example dialogue

Logo → app-icon replacement (pp. 6–7) shows common delivery technology across different benefit mechanisms. The nine-pattern table carries the definitions. The company-value graphics are dated motivation, not current facts or proof of welfare.

> Student: “It has an app, so is that its business model?”
> Coach: “Name whose problem it solves and how. Then identify who might pay, why, and which costs need covering.”

> Student: “A marketplace can only be BM1?”
> Coach: “It may reduce search effort and also expand sellers' online reach. Explain the specific benefit before choosing a label.”

These are constructed teaching dialogues, not actual user answers.

## Ambiguities and correction rules

- BM5/Learning Apps and BM8/Digital Content are shorter source labels, not additional categories.
- Local social reputation in BM2 is related to but not identical with transaction verification in Chapter i.
- A subscription can capture revenue; its presence does not establish profit or retention.
- Federated learning is named, not technically developed; raw-data locality alone is not an absolute privacy proof.
- No observed weaknesses. Begin with one concrete classification/creation-capture case, await the user's attempt, and record only their actual response.
